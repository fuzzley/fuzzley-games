# Card atlases: build them at build time, or commit them as WebP

Research for strategies 3 and 4 of the repo-size plan, measured on 2026-11-21 on the 24-core devbox (i7-13700K, WSL2, Node 26.13.0) in the worktree at 305edc24. No product code was changed and nothing was committed. Every figure here is printed by a script in [`../scripts/atlases/`](../scripts/atlases/) into [`../data/atlases/`](../data/atlases/); each section names the file. Generator runs happened in scratch mirrors outside the repository, so the tracked atlases were never rewritten.

## Summary

- **Strategy 4, now: commit the atlas pages as lossless WebP, with `exact: true`.** They come to 9.17 MiB against the PNGs' 16.55 MiB over HEAD's 21 distinct page blobs, 55.4% of the size, and decode to exactly the PNGs' pixels. Phaser 4.2.1 drew a WebP page identically to its PNG in headless Chrome's WebGL renderer, at full size and shrunk. Every browser the build targets decodes WebP. A full rebuild would add 7.38 MiB less to history, and the 10 rebuilds since 2026-10-03 would have added about 19.46 MiB less. It is a small change: the generator's output step, the page glob, one spec regex, the test harness's PNG-only image sizing, and the docs that name `.png`.
- **Not lossy WebP.** At q 90 the pages are bigger than lossless (76.1% of PNG) and stray by up to 168 of 255 at card edges. Near-lossless at 80 (50.7%, at most 2 of 255 off) saves only 0.78 MiB more than lossless for a rebuild, and loses the exact pixels.
- **Not PNG optimisation.** oxipng at `-o max` saves 4.0%; sharp's adaptive filtering makes the pages 37% bigger; a 256-colour palette is lossy here.
- **Strategy 3 works, but costs every build about half a minute and gives up a reviewed, byte-stable artefact.** The generator ran in the Dockerfile's `node:26.13.1-alpine3.24` image, and the lockfile already carries the musl binaries. A run takes 32–36 s on the devbox (33 s held to 4 cores) and 36–45 s in the Alpine container (36 s held to 4 CPUs). It is deterministic on one platform but not across platforms: glibc, musl and the machine that built the committed atlases each draw the 0.5x and 0.75x pages of the three sheet decks a few corner pixels differently. CI would test other bytes than Docker ships. It saves all 16.55 MiB a rebuild, where WebP saves 7.38. Neither touches the 52.52 MiB of atlas pages already in history; that is the history rewrite's job.
- **Other images: nothing else shipped is PNG.** The site's and games' screenshots are already WebP. The Solitaire Collection's 41 lossless screenshot originals (20.07 MiB, kept out of the image) would be 71.6% as lossless WebP, 5.70 MiB less a full recapture; their history, 147.98 MiB packed, is nearly three times the atlases'. Leave the archived `docs/` PNGs as they are.
- **If strategy 3 is wanted, keep the 21 JSON files committed and generate only the pages**, behind a `--if-stale` key on the inputs' hash that costs about 12 ms, with an Actions cache in the test and build jobs. Then lint and typecheck need nothing generated.

## 1. How the generator works

`yarn workspace solitaire build:atlas` runs `tools/build-card-atlas.mjs` with the modules in `tools/card-atlas/`:

- **Inputs:** the three card sheets in `src/engine/render/assets/sprites/card/` (`playing_card_assets_large.svg`, `_corner_pips.svg`, `_all_corner_pips.svg`, about 1.7 MB each), `card_placeholders.svg` beside them, and for the `mobile` deck its drawing code in `mobile-deck.mjs` with the bundled `tools/card-atlas/fonts/BarlowCondensed-Bold.ttf`. The card artwork's two backs are cut from the large sheet into every deck. 12 files, 5.16 MiB in all ([`hash-inputs.txt`](../data/atlases/hash-inputs.txt)).
- **Dependencies:** two native packages and no browser. `@resvg/resvg-js` 2.6.2 rasterises each SVG region (`renderAsync`, on libuv's pool, 8 frames at once); `sharp` 0.35.5 (libvips 8.18.7) cuts frames, stamps the card edge (`composite` with `blend: "atop"`), checks every edge was inked, and writes each page (`png({ compressionLevel: 9 })`). There is no packer library: `packPages()` in `atlas-writer.mjs` lays the equal-sized frames out on a grid within 4096 px.
- **Checks it makes:** every deck holds exactly the card frames the engine draws, every card's edge is inked on all four sides, and the mobile deck's layout and suit contrast hold; any failure throws.
- **Options:** `--deck <id>` builds one deck; `--preview` also draws a contact sheet into the gitignored `tools/card-atlas/.preview/`.
- **Outputs:** per deck and density, `card_assets-<n>.png` pages and a Phaser multi-atlas manifest `card_assets_atlas.json`; for `mobile`, `vectors.json`, the SVG of each frame, which `card_deck_vectors.ts` imports dynamically to draw the deck at a board's exact size.
- **What the game needs at runtime: all of it.** `card_deck_atlas.ts` imports all 20 manifests and globs every page (`import.meta.glob("…/atlas/*/*/card_assets-*.png", { query: "?url" })`), the player chooses any of the four decks in settings, and `chooseCardAtlas()` picks the density from the board's size. A player downloads only the one or two pages their deck and screen need, but the build must hold every one.

### sharp and resvg on linux-musl x64

- **The lockfile has every musl binary:** `@img/sharp-linuxmusl-x64@0.35.5`, `@img/sharp-libvips-linuxmusl-x64@1.3.4` and `@resvg/resvg-js-linux-x64-musl@2.6.2`, each with `conditions: os=linux & cpu=x64 & libc=musl`. `.yarnrc.yml` sets no `supportedArchitectures`, so Yarn installs the binaries for the platform it runs on: on the Docker stage, the musl ones. Since `sharp` and `@resvg/resvg-js` are the Solitaire Collection's devDependencies and the stage installs everything, **every Docker build already installs them today**. sharp already runs there too: `@fz/style`'s `icons()` plugin, which `fuzzleyStyle()` adds to every game's `gameConfig()` and to the site's config, draws `favicon.ico` and `apple-touch-icon.png` with it in each build. resvg runs in no build today ([`consumers.txt`](../data/atlases/consumers.txt)).
- **The generator runs there.** [`docker-alpine.sh`](../scripts/atlases/docker-alpine.sh) ran the generator in `node:26.13.1-alpine3.24@sha256:6bd9ab43…`, the Dockerfile's pinned image, in a throwaway container fed and emptied through a tar on stdin and stdout (dind can't bind-mount the devbox's files). It installed the lockfile's versions with npm rather than `yarn install --immutable` of the whole frontend, to keep the run cheap; npm resolved the same three musl packages (listed in [`run-alpine.txt`](../data/atlases/run-alpine.txt)). The container was removed with `--rm` and the image, absent before, was removed after.

## 4. A design for strategy 3

1. **Gitignore only the pages** (`frontend/games/solitaire/.gitignore`: `src/engine/render/assets/sprites/atlas/**/card_assets-*.png`, plus the stamp below), and keep the 20 manifests and `vectors.json` committed. They are 0.51 MiB, change only when frames move, and came out identical on all three platforms, so `git status` stays clean after a generate. Lint, typecheck and Prettier then need nothing generated.
2. **A staleness key.** The generator gains `--if-stale`: hash its own files, the card sheets, the installed versions of sharp, libvips, resvg and their platform packages, and the platform with its C library; compare with a gitignored stamp beside the pages; exit if they match and every page each manifest names exists. [`hash-inputs.mjs`](../scripts/atlases/hash-inputs.mjs) prototypes the key: 12 files, 5.16 MiB, a median of 12.3 ms ([`hash-inputs.txt`](../data/atlases/hash-inputs.txt)). The platform has to be in it, since glibc and musl draw different bytes. Regenerating every time instead would add 32–45 s to every `build`, `test` and `dev`, for nothing.
3. **A lock.** `--if-stale` takes an exclusive lock file (`open` with `wx`, waiting while another holds it), checks the stamp again once it has it, and writes the stamp last, so a run killed halfway leaves the pages stale rather than trusted.
4. **Hooks:** solitaire's `build`, `dev`, `start`, `test`, `test:coverage` and `capture:screenshots` start with `node tools/build-card-atlas.mjs --if-stale &&`. `build:atlas` keeps generating unconditionally.
5. **CI:** in the `test` job's solitaire part and in `build`, an `actions/cache` step on `frontend/games/solitaire/src/engine/render/assets/sprites/atlas/**/card_assets-*.png` and the stamp, keyed on `hashFiles()` of the tools, the card sheets and `frontend/yarn.lock`, after the frontend is set up and before the tests or the build run. A hit leaves `--if-stale` to skip; a miss costs about 33 s and saves the cache. `lint` and `typecheck` need nothing. The Docker build always generates, about 36 s on 4 CPUs.
6. **Docs:** `NOTICE`, the two skills, and `.dockerignore`.

**How a developer knows the output is stale:** they don't need to. Every command that reads the pages runs `--if-stale` first, so an edit to an SVG, a tool, or an upgrade of sharp or resvg regenerates on the next `yarn dev`, `test` or `build`. The cost is the 12 ms check, or about half a minute when it regenerates.

**Risks:**

- **What CI tests is not what ships, byte for byte.** CI's specs and build use glibc pages; the image ships musl ones, a few corner pixels apart on 6 of the 24 pages. Harmless to the eye, but no check can compare a build's pages with CI's by hash.
- **A dependency update can change the shipped art with nothing to review.** Today a rebuild is a visible diff a person commits. With build-time generation, a Dependabot bump of sharp or resvg that changes rendering ships straight to `live`. Mitigation: a spec that decodes the generated pages and checks a few frames against tolerances, or a committed per-page pixel digest with a tolerance; both are work.
- **Every Docker build and Cloud Build pays for a generate:** about 36 s on 4 CPUs, 44–45 s on 24, and peak memory near 1 GB on the devbox (485–496 MiB was sampled in Alpine). Cloud Build's machine type is not in the repository, so its time there is unknown.
- **First run and fresh clones** wait about half a minute before the first `yarn dev` or `test`.
- **History is untouched:** the 52.52 MiB already packed stays until the history is rewritten.

## 5. Phaser and WebP

- **Phaser 4.2.1** is the lockfile's (`phaser@npm:^4.2.1`), and the Solitaire Collection is the only game that loads image files: `loader.multiatlas()` in `card_deck_atlas.ts`. No other game's source calls the loader, and `@fz/phaser`'s own `load.` calls are audio ([`consumers.txt`](../data/atlases/consumers.txt)).
- **The loader takes any format the browser decodes.** In `frontend/node_modules/phaser/src/loader/`, `MultiAtlasFile.js` makes an `ImageFile` from each manifest texture's `image` (lines 123–127). `ImageFile.js` fetches it as a blob (`responseType: 'blob'`, line 68) and hands it to an `Image` through `File.createObjectURL()` (line 132), which sets `image.src = URL.createObjectURL(blob)` (`File.js` line 587). The `'image/png'` it passes is only the fallback type for a data URL where `URL` doesn't exist, and the `'png'` extension (line 43) only names a URL for a file given by key alone. `load.atlas()` and `load.image()` take the same `ImageFile`.
- **WebGL upload is the same for both.** Phaser uploads the decoded `Image` with `UNPACK_PREMULTIPLY_ALPHA_WEBGL` on (`WebGLTextureWrapper.js`'s `pma` defaults to true, line 174; set in `WebGLGlobalWrapper.js` line 946), so the browser premultiplies whatever it decoded. Lossless WebP decodes to the same RGBA as the PNG, so the texture is the same. Without `exact`, libwebp may rewrite the colour of fully transparent texels, which premultiplying zeroes anyway; with `exact: true` even those are kept.
- **No colour management either way.** All 24 pages hold only `IHDR`, `pHYs`, `IDAT` and `IEND` chunks: no `gAMA`, `cHRM`, `sRGB` or `iCCP` ([`consumers.txt`](../data/atlases/consumers.txt)). sharp writes no ICC profile into a WebP unless asked.
- **Canvas renderer:** `@fz/phaser`'s host asks for `Phaser.AUTO`, so a browser without WebGL falls back to the canvas renderer, which draws the same `Image` with `drawImage`.
- **Nothing reads texture pixels** outside specs and testing helpers: no `getPixel`, `getPixelAlpha`, pixel-perfect hit tests or `getImageData`.
- **Browser targets:** no `build.target` and no browserslist anywhere in the repository, so Vite 8.3.8's default applies: Chrome 111, Edge 111, Firefox 114, Safari 16.4, iOS 16.4 ([`consumers.txt`](../data/atlases/consumers.txt)). WebP with alpha, lossless included, has been in Safari since 14, Firefox since 65 and Chrome since 32 (caniuse; not checked in this session), so every target decodes it, and nothing in the repository aims older. The site and games already ship 135 WebP files from `public/`.

### Drawn in Chrome

[`phaser-webp-check.mjs`](../scripts/atlases/phaser-webp-check.mjs) serves Phaser's own `dist/phaser.min.js` and HEAD's `classic/1x` page (3876 × 1260) as PNG and as five WebP encodings, loads each through `load.multiatlas()` with a parsed manifest whose `image` is the WebP, as the game does, draws it over felt green in headless Chrome 157 with each renderer, at 1x and shrunk to 0.7 (bilinear, mixing transparent texels in), and reads the pixels back ([`phaser-webp-check.txt`](../data/atlases/phaser-webp-check.txt)):

Decoding costs more: an image element took a median of 25.1 ms to decode the PNG and 40.0 ms the lossless WebP (7 runs each, fresh object URLs). That is once per page as a deck loads, here on the 1x page; not measured on a phone.

## 6. Measurements

[`measure-encodings.mjs`](../scripts/atlases/measure-encodings.mjs) takes each distinct PNG blob at HEAD in a group, decodes it, re-encodes it every way below, decodes the result and compares it with the original, straight and premultiplied (what WebGL draws); "max" is the largest difference in any channel, of 255. oxipng is the prebuilt v10.2.1 binary from its GitHub release, its archive's SHA-256 checked against the one GitHub lists, run from the scratch folder. sharp 0.35.5, libvips 8.18.7, libwebp 1.6.0.

The encodings: `png-9` is the generator's own (`compressionLevel: 9`); `png-9-adaptive` adds `adaptiveFiltering`; `png-palette` is `palette: true, colours: 256, quality: 100, effort: 10, dither: 0` (sharp's `effort` only applies with a palette); `oxipng-max` is `-o max --strip safe`, and `-alpha` adds `--alpha`; `webp-lossless` is `lossless: true, effort: 6`, `-exact` adds `exact: true`; `webp-near-80` and `-60` are `nearLossless: true` at quality 80 and 60; `webp-q90` and `-q80` are lossy with `alphaQuality: 100, smartSubsample: true`.

## 7. Recommendation for strategy 4

**Atlases: lossless WebP with `exact: true`, effort 6.** It halves what each rebuild adds and what a player downloads, with no pixel changed and no change in what WebGL draws. Near-lossless 80 saves 0.78 MiB more a full rebuild for changes of up to 2 of 255; that buys little and gives up exact pixels, which the specs and any future pixel check can rely on.

The change, all in the Solitaire Collection but the harness:

- **`tools/card-atlas/atlas-writer.mjs`:** write `card_assets-<n>.webp` with `.webp({ lossless: true, effort: 6, exact: true })` in place of `.png({ compressionLevel: 9 })`, name it in the manifest's `image` field, and have `cleanOutput()` remove `.png` and `.webp` pages alike, so the old pages go.
- **`src/engine/render/phaser/deck/card_deck_atlas.ts`:** the glob becomes `card_assets-*.webp`.
- **`test/engine/render/phaser/scene/board_scene.spec.ts`:** `ATLAS_PAGE` matches `.webp`.
- **`@fz/phaser/testing`'s `served_files.ts`:** `pngSize()` sizes an image from a PNG header and gives anything else 0 × 0, so every spec that loads a page through `serveWorkspace` would get empty textures. It needs a WebP reader too: `RIFF`…`WEBP`, then the `VP8X` canvas size (24-bit width − 1 and height − 1 at byte 24), `VP8L` (14-bit fields at byte 21) or `VP8 ` (16-bit fields at byte 26), with a spec, and `pngOfSize()`'s callers unchanged.
- **`tools/card-atlas/preview.mjs`** reads `texture.image` through sharp, which reads WebP: no change.
- **Docs:** `NOTICE`'s list of pages, the `solitaire-build` skill ("Pages are PNG, not WebP", which corrected a skill that claimed WebP before the generator wrote it, not a decision against it) and `solitaire-phaser`.
- **Then `yarn build:atlas` once**, and commit the 21 WebP pages with the deletion of the PNGs: one more 9.17 MiB in history, after which each rebuild costs 55% of today's.

Risks: a browser outside the targets without WebP would show no cards, and none is targeted; the encode is slower than PNG's, which adds to `build:atlas` only; a WebP decoder could differ from PNG's by a rounding in the canvas renderer, as measured, which no one sees. Safari and Firefox were not tried.

**Other images: none need converting for the build.** The shipped screenshots are already WebP. The screenshots' lossless originals could be lossless WebP too: `capture-screenshots.mjs` would write `.webp({ lossless: true, effort: 6 })` in place of `.png({ compressionLevel: 9 })` (line 147), and `build-screenshot-thumbs.mjs` read it (`SOURCE`, line 28), saving 5.70 MiB, 28%, on each full recapture; a name other than `overview.webp` would keep it apart from the site's `public/docs/screenshots/<id>/overview.webp`. The other games keep no lossless original and write the site's images straight from the capture, which would save the whole 20.07 MiB a recapture, at the cost of recapturing to change an image's size. The archived `docs/` PNGs are better left: converting them adds new blobs now and frees nothing until the history is rewritten.

**Strategies 3 and 4 together:** if the atlases are generated at build time, writing them as WebP still halves what a player downloads and what the image holds, at the cost of a slower encode in each build (not measured).
