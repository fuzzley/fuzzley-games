# What to listen for

Nobody heard these sounds as they were built. Each was measured and its
spectrum looked at, which catches what is broken and not what is wrong. This
page lists, family by family, the sounds their makers were least sure of,
what each might turn out to be, and what to try first if it is. It is the
place to start on the audition page (`audition/`), and where an ear's
verdict should be written back.

A sound not listed measured as meant; it may still not sound as meant.

## Heard, 2026-11-14

The user listened to all fifteen moments of play and all ninety-eight

  Every doubt listed below for the rocks, the hazards, the pickups, the

## Rocks

| Sound               | It may sound                                             | Try first                                                       |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `break-stone-*`     | like a woodblock's "tok": the small one knocks at 560 Hz | halve the knock's decays, or spread its partials more by take   |
| `break-ice-*`       | louder than stone at the same level, and tiring          | thin the slivers; bring its level down 2 dB                     |
| `fuse-volatile`     | too loud: 5 dB over the other small sounds to the ear    | move it to the level `tick`, or pull its band down              |
| `break-lodestone-*` | like a cartoon wobble                                    | slow the wobble to 6 to 14 Hz, or wobble a filter in its place  |
| `hit-iron`          | piercing when hit again and again: it rings on C6        | lower the anvil to G5                                           |
| `break-boulder`     | weak before its split, with holes between its pieces     | a creak of 60 ms before the snaps; more rumble under the pieces |
| `break-cluster`     | like one steady band at 600 to 900 Hz                    | spread its knocks from 300 to 1,400 Hz                          |

- **Changed because of the instruments:** the first build of the large
  stone, the large lodestone, the boulder and every blast kept 5 to 18% of
  its energy above 300 Hz, so a phone would have lost it. Each now keeps 42
  to 76%: the knocks' upper partials carry them, the blast has a knock in
  its middle, and its thump is a quarter of what it was.
- **A chain of blasts** goes off 90 ms apart: the blast before is about
  13 dB down as the next starts.
- **Longer than the kit's lengths, on purpose:** iron rings on (a large one
  for 0.7 s), and a large crystal for 0.64 s.

## Ship

| Sound                    | It may sound                                     | Try first                                                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `shot-gun`, `shot-rapid` | too much of a retro laser's "pew"                | start the fall at 1.5 kHz, with more air and less tone      |
| `shot-tail`              | metallic: it is mostly the small room's own ring | damp the room more, or two plain dull echoes in its place   |
| `thrust`                 | like plain filtered noise                        | more of its driven band at 500 Hz; a slower, deeper flutter |
| `zap-dash`               | like a hiss over a thump, where it should tear   | more of its low buzz through a sweeping band; less whoosh   |
| `zap-arc`                | sharp: a third to a half of it is above 3 kHz    | lower each take's band by a third                           |
| `pod-hit`                | comic: its tone wobbles 170 cents either way     | 90 cents, or a faster fall                                  |
| `pod-lost`               | like a slide whistle                             | a slower fall, with less vibrato                            |

- **Rapid against the gun.** A level is set by a sound's loudest 50 ms, so
  `shot-rapid`, heard fourteen times a second, measures about 4 dB louder
  as a stream than `shot-gun` at seven. The game should play it 3 dB down.
- **Changed because of the instruments:** `zap-dash`, `pod-hit` and
  `pod-lost` first kept 23 to 30% above 300 Hz and now keep 53 to 70%;
  `zap-arc` was a click with no body and now has a buzz under it; the
  thrust's start and stop lost the lows a phone would never play.
- **The thrust was made again after the ear said it needs work,** three
  ways, and the user chose the second by ear the same day: a throat. It is
  a burn (a driven band of dark noise from 250 to 600 Hz over a soft rush,
  on a shallow breath) through a comb whose pitch wanders 225 to 515 Hz,
  like a stopped pipe that never settles on a note. It loops at exactly two
  seconds, has no oscillator and no line of pitch, keeps under half a
  percent of its energy above 2 kHz, and sits darker than the start and the
  stop, which passed and are unchanged. The loop kept is the very one that
  was heard: its recipe holds the seed it was chosen at.
  - The two not chosen are gone from the recipe: a plain burner, the same
    burn with no throat, and a purr, a warm band of noise opening in soft
    pulses about 27 times a second. Either is a few lines to make again if
    the throat tires in play: its maker thought it the likeliest of the
    three to be heard as a sweep that repeats every two seconds.

  note into three; Rapid stutters one note five times; Lance is one clean
  rising line; Shield closes in pairs from the outside; Magnet is two notes

  and a half, as a long run of sparks would raise it, is 18 ms with nothing

## Voice and rescues

All of it passed the ear. What their maker doubted, for the record:

| Sound         | It might have sounded                                | Would have tried                     |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| every call    | like a synth's "wah", not a creature                 | wider or narrower resonances         |
| every call    | loud for its level: it sits where the ear is keenest | play the voice 3 dB down in the game |
| `voice-yay`   | warbling: its trill is 55 cents either way at 15 Hz  | half the depth                       |
| `voice-cheer` | rough: two of its voices meet on one note            | spread their notes                   |
| `crew-loose`  | sharp for its 45 ms                                  | a lower low-pass                     |

- **How the voice is made:** a narrow pulse with a little saw and a breath
  of noise, on a pitch that glides without overshooting, through three
  resonances that follow a path of mouth shapes, a child's vowels raised for
  a small throat. It first measured as a whistle, its second and third
  harmonics 13 to 25 dB under the first; they now sit 2 to 12 dB under.
- **The voice plays 3 dB down in the game,** by its maker's figures: at the
  level `medium` a call measures about 3 dB louder to the ear than a struck
  sound at the same level.

## Beds

Four passed the ear as they were: `bed-iron-reach`, `bed-storm`,
`bed-shimmer` and `bed-pulse`, "the best one". Two were made again from
what the ear said of both: "Tone is too difficult to listen to for a long
time, needs to be softer and maybe have more variation."

**Heard again the same day:** the tones "sound good", but both beds'
"'wind' sounds sound too much like static hisses. They need to be
softer/quieter or less drawn out maybe." Their air was made again for
that, as the last point below says.

**Heard a third time, the same day:** "they are both good now." So all
ninety-eight sounds have passed the ear on the audition page.

- **What was taken to be wrong.** The beds that passed are made of noise
  and of things that come and go: far metal rings, wind, soft mallet notes.
  The two that failed held steady pure tones in the upper range: the Inner
  belt's sines at C6 and G6 over beating partials, Endless's seven glass
  tones from G5 to E7. A held pure tone between 1 and 3 kHz is what tires
  an ear over minutes.
- **What both are now.** Loops of 32 seconds, twice as long. Nothing held
  above 620 Hz. A soft pad of sines whose upper partials fall away fast,
  with no drive, and beats so slow one takes 16 to 32 seconds. Dulled air
  in a low band that wanders, differently in each ear. And in place of the
  held high tones, a few muted bells in key, far apart and never on a grid,
  as the pulse the ear liked is made of struck notes.
- **`bed-inner-belt`:** C and G; then A and E come in over the held C from
  4 to 18 s; then C, G and D from 14 to 29 s; then home. Seven bells.
- **`bed-endless`:** a deep C that barely moves, and a second voice that
  wanders through G, A and E far below where the glass tones were, each
  note leaning to one ear, with three seconds of C alone before it comes
  round. Five bells in a longer room.

| Sound            | It may sound                                                         | Try first                                |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| `bed-inner-belt` | like a slow tune: seven bells in 32 s is near a melody               | take two of the bells out                |
| `bed-inner-belt` | still: its low C holds through every change                          | let the bass move, at the cost of a dip  |
| `bed-endless`    | too forward for "deep": its second voice sits 10 dB under the C      | 3 dB less of it                          |
| `bed-iron-reach` | like an organ: its drone is plain sines (it passed the ear as it is) | a little drive, or a slow drift of pitch |
| `bed-pulse`      | nagging, at steady eighths (it passed the ear as "the best one")     | drop the off-beats                       |

- **The air, after the second listen.** It was pink noise in a band round
  1.1 kHz (700 Hz in Endless), open to 3.6 kHz, never under a sixth of its
  level, 28 dB down: a steady hiss. It is now a low breath: a band round
  520 Hz (380 in Endless) with nothing above 1.2 kHz (900 Hz), 10 dB
  quieter, which comes in slow swells and is silent between them, for
  about half the loop. This is the air that passed the ear.
- **Each loop's seam** was drawn twice end to end and shows nothing; the
  level is level across it within 0.2 dB. Summed to one ear, a bed loses a
  quarter of a decibel.
- **A phone hears 5% of each** now that their air is low, which is the
  kit's allowance for a bed: the bells are what a phone keeps.
