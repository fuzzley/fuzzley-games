# Work log: L5, the fuzzball's faces and fur

L5 is `implementation-plan.md`'s §4 lift 3 and §6's L5, as C16 settles it:
the fuzzball's nine faces, the look that shifts its eyes, and its fur combed
by force and by static, as plain geometry in `@fz/style/fuzzball`, beside
the mark they are drawn on, ported from the art kit's `lib/fuzzball.mjs`;
drawn as commands by `@fz/phaser/fuzzball`, whose `combedStrands()` keeps
its signature for the brick games and gains a lean; and drawn in SVG by
`@fz/ui`'s `<fz-fuzzball>`, which takes `face` and `lift` inputs. Brick
Breaker and Brick Fuzz Pop keep their looks. L6 draws the pilot from it and
P1 the face cam. The branch is `abu/l5`, in `.claude/worktrees/abu-l5`, cut
from `asteroid-breaker-uplift` at `2099d621`, with L0's alpha on every
command.

Before the first edit, Asteroid Breaker was built and its `dist/` kept in
the scratchpad as the "before" of §9's frame budget.

## Commit 1: the faces, the look and the fur as geometry in `@fz/style`

### What it did

- **`@fz/style/fuzzball`** ports the kit's fuzzball as plain geometry on
  the mark's grid about its body's middle, `{ x, y }` points
  (`GridPoint`), the shape `@fz/phaser/shapes`' `Point` has, so a canvas
  takes it as it is:
  - **The faces:** `FuzzballFace`, its nine names, `FUZZBALL_FACES` in the
    sheet's order, and `eyesOf()`, a face's marks (`EyeMark`: an ellipse, a
    polygon or a round-ended stroke, each in the eyes' ink or the glints'
    white), the eyes' marks first and their glints over them, so the rest
    face is the mark's own eyes and glints number for number, in the order
    the library has always drawn them.
  - **The look:** a `FuzzballLook` says how far a look shifts the eyes.
    `FUZZBALL_LOOK` is today's, 1.3 across and 0.9 down, from where the mark
    has them, as the brick games move their eyes' frames; `FUZZBALL_LOOK_FAR`
    is the kit's, 1.9 and 1.35, taking the eyes about the body's middle as
    it looks, for a caller that asks for more. `lookOffset()` is how far a
    look shifts them, for a scene that moves the eyes' frame, and
    `restingLook()` where a face looks unless told (a plead looks up).
  - **The fur:** `Strand`, `strandsOf()` and `FUZZBALL_STRANDS` move here
    from `@fz/phaser/fuzzball`, the same code; `combFur()` combs them for a
    `FurPose` as the kit's `combFur()` does: static's step (`lift`, 0 to
    `MOST_LIFT`, 3), a push (`lean`), a fright (`puff`), glass round it
    (`within`) and the small form; `sweptFur()` sweeps any fur by a push
    alone, for `combedStrands()`'s lean; and `sparksOf()` gives static's
    sparks at its last step, the kit's ticks and stars, with `SPARK_LOOK`,
    how they are drawn.
  - **The pose:** `fuzzballShape()` poses a face, a look and fur together,
    with the kit's rules: the small form's fur wider (`SMALL_FUR_WIDTH`),
    sparks from `SPARKS_FROM` (2.5) and never in the small form, and how far
    it reaches. `fuzzballForm()` is the form a size takes, the small one at
    `SMALL_UNDER` (12) CSS pixels across the grid and under, as the kit
    draws it.
  - **SVG:** `fuzzballDrawing()` gives a pose as the parts a page's own
    `<svg>` binds, on the mark's view box to a hundredth, as the mark's own
    numbers are; `fuzzballSvg()` takes a `face`, and with none writes the
    file it always wrote, byte for byte.
- **Docs:** `frontend/lib/style/AGENTS.md`'s `src/fuzzball.ts`, and the
  `fuzzley-ui` skill's row for the fuzzball anywhere else (an addition to a
  shared file, §7.3).

### Choices the plan left open

- **Static's fur is the kit's own,** every other strand taller, sparking
  with ticks between the tips and stars, rather than `combedStrands()`'s
  even comb and its five zigzags. `plan.md`'s "Where the code goes" leaves
  the choice to slice 4; the sheet is what the user chose, and
  `combedStrands()` stays the brick games' comb, so their looks hold.
- **The sparks are data, not drawn from a seed.** The kit draws them from
  `seeded()`, which is `@fz/core`'s `createRandom()`, but `@fz/style`
  imports no `@fz/core`, and a spark that moves from one drawing to the
  next is a choice for a scene to make. So the three jumps are laid out
  once, from the seed the sheet's "x4: on end, sparking" was drawn at (4),
  to a thousandth, and follow the fur's tips wherever it is combed.
- **The look keeps its default and lets a caller ask for more,** as the
  brief says: `FUZZBALL_LOOK` stays the brick games', and the kit's is
  `FUZZBALL_LOOK_FAR`, which also draws the eyes about the body's middle
  as a look grows, as the kit's `eyesOf()` does, since the mark's eyes sit
  a unit right of it.
- **The static step is `lift`, from 0 to 3,** the kit's charge by the
  brief's name: whole steps are ×1 to ×4, and between two steps the fur is
  between them, for a scene that eases it. A step past either end is the
  nearest, as the kit's `ramp()` clamps it.
- **One file.** The geometry first went in files beside `fuzzball.ts`,
  imported with their `.ts`, as the library's rule says; the root's
  typecheck failed in every app (TS5097), since an app compiles the page's
  files with its own options, which allow no `.ts` in an import, while Node
  loads `fuzzball.ts` as it is for `src/build/vite.ts`'s icons, which needs
  it. No relative import suits both, so the fuzzball is one file, and its
  header and `AGENTS.md` say why.

### Figures

A scratch script compared the port with the kit's `lib/fuzzball.mjs` pose by
pose: the fur at every step, between two, past the last, at a thrust, a
dash, a fright, in the small form and inside glass, all to the last bit; the
eyes of every face at seven looks and four forms to 4e-15; and the sparks to
0.004 of a grid unit, as their data is to a thousandth. The specs hold the
kit's numbers, copied in to a ten-thousandth.

### The review

A fresh `general-purpose` agent read the staged change against the kit,
the sheet and the plan. It found the fur, the eyes, the sparks, the pose,
the form and `fuzzballSvg()`'s default output sound, and the one-file
reasoning right. Its findings, and how each was answered:

1. **`fuzzballDrawing()` said nothing of how far it reaches.** Lifted,
   puffed or swept fur and every spark lie past the mark's view box, which
   an `<svg>` clips. Taken: the drawing has a `reach`, its fur's and its
   sparks' with their halos, and its doc says what draws it must let them
   show past the box, the body never growing (which `<fz-fuzzball>` does,
   in commit 3).
2. **The specs held only each face's first mark to the kit,** so a lid
   drooping the wrong way on the right, a spiral wound one way on both, or
   a glint on the wrong side would pass. Taken: every mark of every face,
   glints too, is held to the kit's numbers in a few words each (its kind,
   tone, middle and radii, or its points' count, ends and width), and the
   small form's too.
3. **`SPARK_LOOK` lacked the kit's least core width,** 0.8 of a pixel,
   which wins below about 46 pixels across the grid, and said nothing of
   round ends or of the core's color. Taken: `tickCoreLeast`, round ends
   and corners in its doc, and the core's mix, toward the text by 0.4 in a
   light palette and toward white by 0.82 in a dark one (`coreMix`), the
   kit's `sparkTones()`, as data each drawer takes.
4. **`sparksOf()`'s `@throws` didn't hold** for fur one strand short.
   Taken: it checks the count first; a spec tries the small form's and one
   short.
5. **`strandsOf()` read a doubled space as a 0.** Taken: it splits on any
   run of spaces and refuses a strand of five numbers, as the kit does.
6. **Small ones.** `fuzzballSvg()` took the eyes from a whole drawing;
   it now takes them from `eyesOf()`. An SVG mark said `glint: boolean`
   where a grid mark says `tone`; both say `tone`. `JUMPS`' doc opened in
   two sentences, the header's "with nothing but" didn't read, and
   `GridPoint` said it lay from the body's middle while `aboutBody()` takes
   a point of the view box: each reworded.

None reshaped the change, so there was no second review.

## Commit 2: the faces, the look and the fur as commands in `@fz/phaser`

### What it did

- **`@fz/phaser/fuzzball`** draws commit 1's geometry:
  - **The faces and the look:** `eyesCommands()` takes an `EyesPose`
    (`@fz/style`'s: a face, a look, a range, and the small form and how
    tiny) after its
    style, and with none draws the mark's eyes as it always has, command
    for command, which a spec holds: each eye an ellipse's points, each
    glint a dot. A face's other marks are a polygon each, or a line round
    at its ends and corners (`stroke()`, opaque, as L0 says). A glint is
    drawn where it is a device pixel across or more at its narrowest,
    the half a pixel's radius it always had, now for the set face's
    glints too, which are polygons.
  - **Fur by force:** `combedStrands()` keeps its share and gains a lean,
    a push on the grid, through which it sweeps the combed fur as
    `@fz/style`'s `sweptFur()` does; with none, the fur is what it was.
  - **Fur by static:** `fuzzballCommands()` and `fuzzballWearing()` take a
    `furWidth`, for the small form's wider strands, and `furReach()` a
    width, and they draw `combFur()`'s fur as any other.
    `staticSparkCommands()`, beside the brick games' `sparkCommands()` in
    `combing.ts`, draws `sparksOf()`'s sparks in a `SparkStyle`, a core,
    a halo and the pixel ratio, as `SPARK_LOOK` says and the kit draws them, as
    `StaticSparks`: the halos, opaque, a tick's a line round at its ends
    and corners and a star's the star with a line round its edge, which a
    game bakes in a frame of their own and fades to
    `SPARK_LOOK.haloAlpha` as one, as L0 says of a drawing meant at part
    strength; and the cores over them, a tick's never under
    `SPARK_LOOK.tickCoreLeast` CSS pixels, as the kit's sheet draws it.
  - **Moved:** `Strand`, `strandsOf()`, `FUZZBALL_STRANDS`, `aboutBody()`
    and `FUZZBALL_LOOK` are `@fz/style`'s, handed on by name, so every
    import of them holds; their specs are `@fz/style`'s, and a spec here
    holds each to be the same one.
- **Docs:** `frontend/lib/phaser/AGENTS.md`'s `src/fuzzball/`, and a
  sentence added to the `phaser-core` skill's `@fz/phaser/fuzzball` bullet
  (an addition to a shared file, §7.3).

### Choices the plan left open

- **No drawing of a whole posed fuzzball in one call.** A game bakes its
  body and fur in one frame and its eyes in another, which its scene moves
  as it looks, so the entry keeps those as two calls, and the sparks a
  third, their halos a frame of their own. `fuzzballShape()` gives the
  three their parts from one pose.
- **Every halo beneath every core.** The kit draws each spark's halo and
  then its core, so a star's halo may lie over its tick's core; here all
  the halos are one frame beneath all the cores, which shows only where a
  star's halo touches its tick, and where two halos meet they show no
  stronger than one.
- **The sparks' colors are the caller's.** `SPARK_LOOK.coreMix` says how
  the kit mixes the core, toward the text in a light palette and toward
  white in a dark one, but `@fz/phaser` holds no palette, so L6 mixes it
  from its own inks.

### The review

A fresh `general-purpose` agent read the staged change against the kit,
the shapes and how a frame draws them. It found the rest face's commands
and `combedStrands(share)` without a lean the same as before, command for
command, the moved names held, and L0's rules kept. Its findings, and how
each was answered:

1. **The sparks' halos weren't the kit's.** A polyline is drawn with the
   canvas's mitred joins, so each tick's halo spiked up to two units past
   its sharp bends where the kit's round join reaches 0.75, its ends were
   square, and the star's points pushed out left its halo thin along the
   arms. Taken, as it suggested, by L0's other rule: `staticSparkCommands()`
   gives `StaticSparks`, the halos opaque and drawn as the kit draws them,
   round at every end and corner, for a frame a game fades as one, and the
   cores. The approximations and their specs went.
2. **`furReach()` took no width,** so a frame sized by it for the small
   form was short by 0.35 of a unit. Taken: it takes the strands' width.
3. **The glint spec would have passed on the widest side.** Taken: it
   tries the set face at 0.8, its glints wider than a pixel but not as
   tall.
4. **The no-lean spec checked nothing,** both calls running the same code.
   Taken: dropped, as the value specs already hold the fur with no lean.
5. **The faces' order spec took its expected list from what it checked,**
   so a face that lost its eyes would pass. Taken: it counts each mark's
   commands, one for an ellipse or a polygon and a line's pieces and dots.
6. **Small ones.** Taken: the spark drawers sit together in `combing.ts`,
   each saying which sparks it draws, and with them the helpers whose `by`
   was a distance went; the log names `tiny`. A circle's glint measured
   across rather than by its radius can differ only at a rounding on the
   threshold, so it stays.

The halos' answer reshaped the sparks' API, so the change had a second
review, of the answers and what the first missed. It found them right, and:

1. **A tick's least core was in device pixels,** where the kit's sheet,
   and `@fz/style`'s sizes, count CSS pixels, so on a phone of two or three
   device pixels to one a small fuzzball's cores would be half or a third
   as wide as the kit's. Taken: `SparkStyle` carries the pixel ratio, which
   a caller must give, and `SPARK_LOOK`'s doc says CSS pixels; a spec tries
   two to one.
2. **A spark's kind was told by a ternary,** so a new kind would draw as a
   star unnoticed. Taken: a `switch`, which lint holds to every kind.
3. **A glint was kept or dropped command by command,** so a glint drawn
   as a line could lose its round ends and keep its pieces. No face has
   one; taken all the same: a glint is kept whole or not at all.
4. **The `phaser-core` skill didn't say the halos come back opaque.**
   Taken: it says they want a frame of their own, faded, and names
   `eyesCommands` and `staticSparkCommands` among its triggers.
5. **Small ones.** Taken: `furWidth`'s doc; `combing.ts` takes the strands
   from `@fz/style` as it takes the rest, and names the kit's file in its
   header; the opacity spec asks for no alpha rather than reading a joined
   string.

The second review's answers corrected the change rather than reshaping
it, so there was no third. `SPARK_LOOK`'s doc is `@fz/style`'s, so its
checks and its users' ran too.
