# Work log: L0, alpha

L0 is the first part of `implementation-plan.md`'s §4 lift 4, and §6's L0:
an optional `alpha` on every `DrawCommand` in `@fz/phaser/shapes`, which
`atlasFrameOf()` hands the `Graphics` and `svgOf()` writes, since the art
L1, L3, L4, L5 and L6 port from `art/lib/*.mjs` draws cracks, glints,
auras and halos at part strength. The wash (`WashCommand`, `PaintCommand`)
and the canvas painter are L3's, and aren't here. The branch is `abu/l0`,
in `.claude/worktrees/abu-l0`, cut from `asteroid-breaker-uplift` at
`da697c6e`.

## Commit 1: `feat(phaser): draw every shape at an optional alpha, in the atlas and in SVG`

### What it did

- **The commands.** Every command and both styles extend one interface of
  `shapes.ts`'s own, `Alpha`, whose `alpha?` is a share from 0, unseen, to
  1, opaque, as a command without it is. It is left out, never set to
  `undefined`.
- **The builders** take it from the style, as they take the color: `rect()`,
  `roundRect()`, `circle()`, `ring()`, `line()`, `polyline()` and
  `polygon()` each keep their style's `alpha` (through `alphaOf()`, which
  leaves the key out where the style has none). So everything built on them
  carries it with no change of its own: `stroke()`, `frameAround()`,
  `outlineRoundRect()`, `crispOutline()`, `diePips()`, `pipRow()`,
  `dotIn()`, `discIn()` and `rimIn()`. `symbolLineStyle()` was the one
  builder that rebuilt a style field by field, so it now spreads the style
  it is given, keeping its alpha.
- **The transforms** keep it: `shifted()` and `scaledBy()` spread the
  command in every case already, and `centered()` and `bounded()` go through
  `shifted()`. `placed()` and `turned()` work on points, which have none.
- **`atlasFrameOf()`** hands the command's alpha to `fillStyle()` or
  `lineStyle()`; `undefined` takes Phaser's own default of 1, so an opaque
  command writes the same command buffer as before.
- **`svgOf()`** writes it as `fill-opacity` on what fills and
  `stroke-opacity` on what strokes, through `fillOf()` beside `strokeOf()`,
  and nothing for an opaque shape, so every drawing without alpha is
  written byte for byte as before.
- **The docs:** `frontend/lib/phaser/AGENTS.md`'s `src/shapes/` and
  `src/scene/` paragraphs say what alpha is, where it comes from, what it is
  for and what a drawing at part strength as a whole takes instead, and how
  `svgOf()` and `atlasFrameOf()` draw it; the `phaser-core` skill's
  paragraph on `shapes` gains the style's `alpha` (an addition to a shared
  file, §7.3).

### Choices the plan left open

- **Alpha lives on the style,** `{ color, alpha: 0.4 }`, as Phaser's own
  `fillStyle(color, alpha)` and `lineStyle(width, color, alpha)` take it,
  rather than as another argument to every builder. A builder's signature
  stays as it was, and every builder above it carries the alpha for nothing.
- **No helper that fades a drawing.** The kit's drawings at part strength
  as a whole (`place(…, { opacity })`, `seen(opacity)`) are SVG groups
  under `opacity`, which blend once: a burr's thorns lie over outlines in
  the field's color, and an orb's glass over a disc in it (`things.mjs`),
  which a fade shape by shape would show through. So such a drawing is
  baked at full strength and its image shown at the alpha, with
  `setAlpha()`, as the game's debris, trail and hazard painters already show
  theirs, and `AGENTS.md` and the `phaser-core` skill say so. A command's
  own alpha is for one shape at part strength in its frame, a crack, a
  glint or a halo, given through its style; where a game has a command and
  wants it fainter,
  `{ ...command, alpha }` is all it takes.
- **`fill-opacity` and `stroke-opacity`, not `opacity`.** Each command is
  one paint, a fill or a stroke, so the two look alike; the paint's own
  attribute says which, as Phaser's fill and line styles do, and costs the
  browser no group to composite.
- **No clamp.** The builders clamp sizes because a canvas throws at a
  negative radius; nothing throws at an alpha past 0 or 1. The atlas is
  drawn by Phaser's canvas renderer (`generateTexture()`), which writes it
  into an `rgba()` that CSS clamps, and SVG clamps opacity too, so a
  strength a port works out below 0, such as the kit's
  `0.8 * (1 - s / 0.6)`, draws as the kit drew it.
- **Overlaps are said, not mended.** Each shape blends on its own, so
  where two at part alpha overlap, the overlap shows stronger, unlike SVG's
  `opacity` on a group, which composites the group once. Two builders make
  shapes that overlap, and their docs and `AGENTS.md` say what to draw at
  part alpha instead. `stroke()`'s lines and dots meet at every point, so a
  line at part alpha is one shape, a polyline or a `bandAlong()` band; a
  canvas strokes a path once, so a polyline at part alpha shows even.
  `frameAround()`'s bars overlap at the corners, so a frame at part alpha is
  the band of `outlineRoundRect()` about the area inset by half the width,
  with no radius: one polygon, with no overlap and no seam. Both stay as
  they were, so no drawing of today's changes.

### The review

A fresh `general-purpose` agent read the staged change. It found opaque
drawing unchanged in the atlas's command buffer and in `svgOf()`'s text,
and the builders, the transforms, the `Alpha` interface, the specs and the
lack of a clamp sound. Its findings, and how each was answered:

1. **`faded()` was offered as the port of the kit's group opacity, and
   isn't one.** The first version had `faded(command, share)` in
   `transforms.ts`, multiplying a command's alpha, which its doc, this log
   and `AGENTS.md` offered for a drawing at part strength. The kit's
   `place(…, { opacity })` is a `<g opacity>` that blends once, and its
   burr and orb stack opaque layers, so a fade shape by shape shows the
   layers beneath through what lies on them. It also wrote
   `alpha: 1` for a share of 1, so `svgOf()` would write `fill-opacity="1"`
   and a spec's `toEqual()` against the builder's own command would fail.
   Checked against the kit and the painters, and taken: `faded()` is gone,
   since §6 L0 doesn't ask for it, and the docs say how a whole drawing is
   shown at part strength instead (above). This reshaped the change, so it
   had a second review.
2. **`frameAround()`'s corners overlap, said only in the log.** The
   reviewer offered its doc, or side bars between the top and the bottom,
   which it thought Brick Breaker's specs might compare. They don't (its
   glow's spec holds the frame's size and colors alone), so the sides first
   ran between the top and the bottom. The second review found that wrong
   (below), and the bars are as they were, with the doc.
3. **Small ones.** `drawShape()`'s doc restated how Phaser defaults an
   alpha; it now says what it draws ("at its alpha, or opaque where it has
   none"). The SVG specs' alpha was 0.4 alone, which held no rounding; they
   now draw at a third and expect `"0.333"`. A `NaN` alpha draws
   differently in the canvas (`rgba(…,NaN)` is ignored, so the fill before
   it stays) and in SVG (opaque): declined, as a `NaN` in any field of a
   command, a radius or a color, misdraws too, and none says so.

### The second review

A fresh `general-purpose` agent read the change again, with the first
review's answers. It found both answers sound and the change otherwise
fine: opaque drawing unchanged, every kind covered, the docs true to the
code. Its findings:

1. **`frameAround()`'s new sides hold only on whole pixels and wide
   areas.** Where `y + width` isn't a whole pixel, the canvas antialiases
   both edges where a side meets the top, leaving a seam (half coverage
   twice is 0.75), which the old full-height sides covered; so opaque
   pixels would change at a fractional frame, and an area under two widths
   still overlaps. Today's one user, Brick Breaker's glow, is whole pixels,
   so it draws the same either way. Taken by undoing the change rather than
   qualifying it: `frameAround()` is as it was, so no drawing of today's
   changes, and its doc and `AGENTS.md` say its corners overlap and that a
   frame at part alpha is `outlineRoundRect()`'s band about the area inset
   by half the width, with no radius: one polygon, with no overlap and no
   seam, the library's own way of drawing an outline.
2. **No spec pinned an alpha of 0,** so `alpha ? { alpha } : {}` in
   `alphaOf()`, or the same in `fillOf()` or `strokeOf()`, would pass and
   draw an unseen shape opaque, which a port's `1 - s ** 3` at its end
   gives. Taken: the builders' spec runs at 0.4 and at 0, and `svgOf()`'s
   writes `fill-opacity="0"` and `stroke-opacity="0"`. Both mutants were
   tried by hand: three specs fail with them, and all pass once restored.
   The run also showed the SVG specs' titles printing the command where the
   attribute's name was meant, so their tables now give the name second.
3. **"Baked opaque" is loose,** as a drawing shown at part strength as a
   whole may hold shapes at part alpha of its own. Taken: "baked at full
   strength", in `AGENTS.md`, the skill and this log.
4. **Lines left long by editing in place,** in `AGENTS.md`, the skill and
   this log. Rewrapped.

None of these reshapes the change: the first takes a fix of the first
review's back out, and the rest are specs and words, so there was no third
review.

## What it left

- **For the look packages.** A shape at part strength in its frame takes
  `alpha` in its style; a drawing at part strength as a whole is baked at
  full strength and its image shown with `setAlpha()`; a line at part alpha
  is a polyline or a `bandAlong()` band, never `stroke()`; a frame at part
  alpha is `outlineRoundRect()`'s band. Nothing clamps an alpha, and none
  needs to: the canvas and SVG both clamp it.
- **For L3,** who owns `src/shapes/` after L0: the wash goes in a
  `PaintCommand` beside `DrawCommand`, as §4 says; `fillOf()` in `svg.ts`
  is where a gradient's `fill` would go.

  `y test --only … --jobs 2 -- --maxWorkers=4` can't run, as
