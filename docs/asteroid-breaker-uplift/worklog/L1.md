### How Phaser lights a rock

Phaser 4.2.1's light is a point light (`DefineLights.glsl`): a face's color
is its texel times the ambient color plus, for each light, the light's
color times its intensity, the cosine between the face's normal and the
way to the light, and a falloff of 1 − d²/r², clamped. Its z is in the
same pixels as its x and y, and the normal map's normals turn with the
image. So the spike's light stood far off: at the field's middle plus the
way to the light times 100 times the field's longer side, its z that way's
z times the same, its radius a thousand times that distance, so its
falloff is above 0.9999 anywhere on the field and its way the same at
every rock. The ambient is a gray, (a, a, a). A face toward the viewer
takes a + i × z, which the spike held to 1, so the outline, the marks and
the cracks, which face the viewer, draw in their own colors. Phaser's
default of ten lights (`render.maxLights`) holds the field's light and
nine more.

The first light was the kit's: its way (`LIGHT`, the unit of −0.52,
−0.62, 0.58, whose z is 0.5826), and its 0.3 and 0.95 over what a face
toward the viewer takes, 0.3 + 0.95 × 0.5826 = 0.853, an ambient of 0.352
and an intensity of 1.113, so faces run from 0.352 to 1.465 times their
ink.

### The contrast, and the look again

The review (below) found the kit's light can't ship: §5.2 holds a rock's
brightest face in a light palette and its darkest in a dark one to 3:1
against the field. The extremes worked out with the contrast spec's own
`contrast()`, over each palette's inks:

- **At the kit's light,** 0.352 to 1.465: Classic's and Soft pastel's
  stone at its brightest takes 1.89, and its ice 1.90; Midnight's iron at
  its darkest takes 1.25, its lodestone 1.26, and Casino night's iron 1.32.
- **What passes:** in a light palette, faces at most 1.15 times their ink
  (stone sets it, at 3.03); in a dark palette, at least 0.725 (Midnight's
  iron sets it, at 3.01).

So the light can't be one for both modes and keep a range. The turns would
meet the same bar, their lit tones mixed less with white and their deep
less with the field, kind by kind; the normal map has one light for every
rock, so the worst kind sets it, and the view can still bake a kind's
facets in another shade of its ink. With a face toward the viewer held to
its ink, a light at a height z takes i = (most − 1) / (1 − z) in a light
palette and i = (1 − least) / z in a dark one, and any height passes; the
height sets the free end. So the spike tried a light for each mode, along
the kit's way across the field, its height chosen so the free end comes
near the kit's: in a light palette the darkest face about half its ink,
as the kit's deep, 52% of the way from its ink to the text, is; in a dark
palette the brightest near the kit's 1.465.

- **Light palettes:** z 0.786, the way (−0.397, −0.474, 0.786), an
  intensity of 0.654 and an ambient of 0.486, so faces run from 0.486 to
  1.14 times their ink: high, as from over the viewer's shoulder.
- **Dark palettes:** z 0.365, the way (−0.598, −0.713, 0.365), an
  intensity of 0.712 and an ambient of 0.74, so faces run from 0.74 to
  1.45: low, raking across the field.

Every kind drawn so, on the dev server at 1200 by 700 at 2×, in Classic
felt and Midnight blue, beside the sheet's fifth row: in Classic the rocks

### The choice

The normal map, by C9's rule: it draws, the same after a lost context; it
keeps the frame within §9's budget, with more room than the turns on the
page's main thread, though it costs the GPU more; and it looks like the
kit within reason under a light that passes the contrast spec. Its
memory, 72 MB at 32 px a unit with the normal map uploaded twice, is about
what eight turns of the whole frames alone take (70 MB), which would need
lift 5's pages besides; twelve turns, or turns of the cracked frames too,
take 105 to 261 MB.

## Commit 2: `feat(asteroid-breaker): port the art kit's rocks, their cut, cracks, light and tones`

### What it did

C10's port, which nothing in the game draws yet: commit 3 draws it.

- **`src/view/art/rocks/`.** `rock_shape.ts` cuts a rock of a kind and
  size from a seed (`makeRock()`), its outline, its rim, its facets and
  each kind's marks, from each kind's cut at each size it comes in
  (`KIND_CUTS`, `cutOf()`), the seed mixed as the kit mixes it
  (`seed × 7919 + kind's length × 131 + size's length`); `cut.ts` the dome,
  its strips, a stone's dents and a crystal's gems; `cracks.ts` the cracks
  for the hits held (`cracksOf()`); `light.ts` how brightly a light falls
  on a facet (`litBy()`) and the color a normal map holds for its way
  (`normalColor()`); and `space.ts` the arithmetic they share, among it
  `around()`, a ring's item counted round it.
- **`src/view/colors/tones.ts`.** The kit's `tonesOf()`, the four tones of
  an ink, mixed with each channel rounded as the kit rounds it
  (`mixTone()`), for the marks.
- **The specs** hold one rock of each kind, cut from the seed 3, the
  sheet's first outline, to the kit's numbers, copied in to four places:
  every kind medium, but the boulder, which comes only as itself; every
  other size of every kind in brief (its outline, its counts, the sums of
  its corners, facings and marks, and its dots); the
  boulder's cracks for five hits; `litBy()` and `normalColor()` for a face
  toward the viewer and three of the boulder's facets at two turns; the
  tones of a stone's ink and a volatile rock's in all four palettes; and
  `createRandom(3)`'s first five draws against the kit's `seeded(3)`.
  Besides, each kind is cut at exactly the sizes the catalog gives it hits
  at, which the compiler holds too (`KIND_CUTS` is typed by the catalog's
  `hits`), every kind at every size is cut in facets within its reach, the
  same rock comes again from the same seed, and the geometry's edges each
  have a spec of their own.
- **`AGENTS.md`** says what `src/view/art/rocks/` and `tones.ts` hold.

Before the specs were written, a scratch spec (deleted) held the port to
the kit itself, imported, for every kind and size at seeds 0 to 7, the
cracks for 0 to 6 hits, the light and the normal color of every facet at
three turns, and every ink's tones and the kit's `toneAt()` across all four
palettes: 137 cases, each equal to the last bit.

### Choices the plan left open

- **`toneAt()` isn't ported.** C10 names it, but it gives a face one of
  the kit's four tones by the light on it, which the normal map leaves to
  the shader (commit 1): a face's color is its ink times `litBy()`. The
  four tones stay, for the marks, which the kit draws in them.
- **The light is handed in.** The kit's `litBy()` defaults to its own
  light; the port's takes a `RockLight` (its way, its ambient and its
  intensity), as the palette will hold one for each mode (commit 1), and
  the spec copies the kit's in.
- **`around()` in place of a fallback on every read of a ring.** The kit
  reads `ring[(i + 1) % n]`, which TypeScript types as maybe missing; a
  fallback at each read was a branch no rock reaches, twenty in all,
  which took the view's branches under their floor of 95% (93.6%). Each
  read goes through `around()`, which throws for an empty ring, so the
  port reads as the kit does, and the view's branches are at their floor
  again.
- **One loop made a step.** `unwrapped()` turned an angle on by whole
  turns while it lay half a turn behind; no ring's corners lie more than a
  turn and a half behind its first, so it adds one turn or none, the same
  numbers.
- **Smaller departures, none reached by a rock:** `normalColor()` clamps
  each channel and takes no turn as none; `cracksOf()` and `strip()`
  throw for a rock or a ring with no corners, through `around()`, where
  the kit seeds the cracks from 0 or reads past the end; and ice's marks
  ask whether a size is large alone, as ice comes in no other big size.
