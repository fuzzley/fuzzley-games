# Work log: R3, the wave

R3 builds `implementation-plan.md`'s §6 R3 in Asteroid Breaker's rules: the
last few rocks of a wave lean toward the pod as a lodestone does, so a
wave's tail is no longer than it was; the three set pieces (C24), each a
new kind of boulder in `ROCK_KINDS`, with their rules, their art on L1's
facets and their sounds; and strays when they can still matter, the first
within 5 s of play on any field and one sent at once, once a wave, to a
pilot with no one in tow. The report times each wave's tail, before and
after. The newest entry is last.

## 2. The last rocks come to the pod

- **Rebased** first, at the coordinator's word, onto
  `asteroid-breaker-uplift` at `d2df10a8` (P1's face cam and S4's voice,
  beds and sound settings), commit 1 becoming `f4af8e35`: no conflict, and
  the report at commit 1 prints byte for byte what it printed before.
- **Did:** `stepRocks()` leans each of a wave's last few toward the ship
  (`leanTowardShip()`): each step its velocity closes a share of the gap
  to its size's most speed, times its kind's and the field's rocks' speed,
  straight at the ship, `LAST_FEW_LEAN` (2) a second at the field's pace,
  so its drift across the way dies away and it comes in, never faster
  than its size flies; a lodestone's own pull adds to it. It leans only
  from beyond `LAST_FEW_NEAR` (12 units), only once the wave has held more
  whole rocks than its last few (`RockField.hadMore`, which the saved
  field keeps), and not until the wave's first 3 s are flown
  (`FAIR_COURSE_MS`), which its layout judges its courses fair over. The
  last few now ride in the fill and the field's setup (`Fill.lastFew`,
  `FieldSetup.lastFew`), so the sound lab's scenes set theirs (0).
  `AGENTS.md`, the content skill and the testing skill say so.
- **Outside R's files:** `tools/sound-lab.ts` (its scene's field setup
  gains `lastFew: 0`, which the compiler asks for), `AGENTS.md` and the two
  skills.
- **Choices,** each from what the reports and the specs showed:
  - **Steered, not pulled.** A lodestone's pull, an acceleration toward
    the ship, was tried first at 1 to 10 units a second each second. At 1
    it did nothing a 2-second tail could see; stronger, it put a rock in
    orbit round the ship, which the shell's specs' plain `shoot()` circled
    forever and a player would have to lead round a curve. Closing the gap
    to a course straight at the ship kills the drift that makes an orbit.
  - **Not near the ship.** Steered right up to the ship, a fast small rock
    at Fast dogged it: two seeds of Fuse and Wildfire at Fast on the narrow
    field never cleared, the autopilot fleeing a rock 2 units behind it
    round the field for four minutes. Within 12 units a rock flies on as
    it flies, which the pilot can dodge and shoot head on; no play of any
    seed has stalled since.
  - **A share of 2 a second.** Tried at ½, 1, 2, 3 and 4 with twelve seeds
    on both fields: 2 shortened the tails most with no play stalled;
    3 and 4 had plays of Whiteout, Shower and Gem at Fast that ran past
    three minutes.
  - **Only once a wave has held more than its last few.** A wave laid as
    its last few, as the shell's specs' one-rock waves and First light on
    the narrow field are, has no hunt to shorten; leaning it broke the
    specs' `endRun()`, whose ship, ramming a leaning rock, caught its crew
    knocked loose again until the rock ran out. First light on the wide
    field, 7 rocks laid as its 7 last few, leans after its first break.
  - **After the wave's first 3 s,** as `liesFairly()` flies a laid rock's
    course without the lean, so a wave that comes to its last few that
    soon keeps the fairness its layout promised.

## 3. Strays when they can still matter

- **Did:** a wave's first stray comes 4 s into its play on any field
  (`STRAY_FIRST_MS`, 4000, once 6000 on 40 by 40 and grown by the field's
  area, so 13.2 s on the widest), and a pilot left with no one in tow and
  none knocked loose to catch again is thrown a lifeline: one stray more,
  sent at once, once a wave, beyond the strays the wave sends, while none
  is adrift (`Strays.lifelineSent`, `stepStrays()`). The wave's own strays
  are counted as before (`Strays.sent`, which the lifeline isn't among, so
  a wave never sends more than it says); a lifeline gone leaves the wave's
  first at its time, and puts any later one off as any stray gone does
  (`nextStrayAfter()`). The saved field keeps `lifelineSent`; a field kept
  agrees with its run when no more strays came home than the wave sent,
  its lifeline among them (`fitsItsRun()`). `Fill.strayFirstMs` went, as
  the time no longer grows by area; `fieldFor()` sets it. How to play
  says a lone pilot is sent one; `AGENTS.md` and the content skill say
  when strays come.
- **Outside R's files:** `src/app/help/help.component.ts` and its spec
  (How to play's line on strays, which "from wave 3" alone would now
  belie), `AGENTS.md` and the content skill.
- **Choices:**
  - **4 s, not 5.** "Within the first 5 s of play": a stray comes in off
    an edge, so 4 s has it on the field by the fifth. Play's time is the
    field's steps, which wait for the first move.
  - **The lifeline is one more,** not the wave's next sent early: a wave
    of no strays (waves 1 and 2) throws one too, and one that has sent
    its own still does, as the design's "a pilot with no one in tow is
    sent one at once, once a wave" reads. It waits while a stray is
    adrift, or one of the crew knocked loose, either of which the pilot
    can catch already, and comes the step the last of them goes.
  - **A stray adrift goes with a reload,** as it always has, the lifeline
    too: the saved field counts it sent, and a pilot taken up alone gets
    no second. Keeping which stray is adrift for this one case wasn't
    worth a field.
  - **The report's strays sent stay the wave's,** so "meeting a stray if
    it sends them" still means its own; the untouchable plays the report's
    home column counts are never hit, so never alone.
