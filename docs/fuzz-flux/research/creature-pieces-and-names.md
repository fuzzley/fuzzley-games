# Creature pieces and a name check for Fuzz Bunch

Notes gathered on 2026-11-25. Read: Wikipedia, All The Tropes, Steam and App
Store pages, reviews (Gamezebo, Pocket Gamer, Pocket-lint, The Next Web, GiN,
Kotaku), Super Mario Wiki, Puyo Nexus, Wiktionary and the Game Accessibility
Guidelines, through a fetch tool that summarizes, so wording is paraphrased
unless quoted. Skipped: all source code, art and audio. TV Tropes, MobyGames,
Giant Bomb, Fandom wikis and Justia refused the tool; facts from them come from
search listings, marked "(listing)". Not read: Fishdom, Frozen Free Fall, Sushi
Cat, Cut the Rope, Chao, Gordo and Frizzle Fraz. Read but left out, as they add
nothing: Kirby's Avalanche and Furby. The web search budget ran out during the
name check; see section 8.

Fuzz Bunch was the design's working title when these notes were written. Section
8 is why it changed, first to Fuzz Trio. On 2026-11-26 the user named the game
Fuzz Flux, which these notes did not weigh. Two general web searches that day
found nothing by that name: the nearest were Fox Flux Deluxe, a puzzle
platformer on itch.io, Fuzzy Flip, a matching game on Steam, and several
games called Flux.

## 1. Chuzzle, the closest precedent

- **Origin.** Released May 12, 2005: developed by Raptisoft Games, designed by
  John Raptis and published by PopCap Games. [Wikipedia: Chuzzle]
- **A live brand.** Raptisoft published Chuzzle 2 itself (iOS and Android,
  December 18, 2018; 13K App Store ratings at 4.7) and Chuzzle Snap (August
  2020). Chuzzle 2's Steam page says "Coming soon". Chuzzle Deluxe is still sold
  on Steam, 94% positive of 1,503 reviews. [Wikipedia: Chuzzle; App Store:
  Chuzzle 2; Steam: Chuzzle 2; Steam: Chuzzle Deluxe]
- **The word "fuzzballs".** Wikipedia calls the pieces "fuzzballs called
  Chuzzles", the word Fuzzley uses for its mascot. [Wikipedia: Chuzzle]
- **Move.** The player drags a whole row or column. Lines wrap, so a piece
  pushed off one edge returns on the other. [Wikipedia: Chuzzle]
- **Board and match.** The board is 6×6 with eight colors (which eight: not
  found). Three or more touching Chuzzles of one color pop, new ones fall from
  the top, and cascades follow. A 2×2 square counts, so a match need not be a
  straight line (inferred). [Wikipedia: Chuzzle; All The Tropes]
- **Super Chuzzle.** Popping five or more at once leaves a flashing piece that
  explodes a 3×3 area when matched. [Wikipedia: Chuzzle; The Next Web]
- **Fat (Giant) Chuzzle.** A 2×2 piece that moves two lines at once and scores
  more. It forms from a cleared 2×2 square [All The Tropes; Pocket Gamer:
  Chuzzle] or from a 2×2 gap after pops [Wikipedia: Chuzzle]; sources disagree.
- **Rainbow Chuzzle.** Worth more points [Pocket Gamer: Chuzzle]: 3,000 each,
  three at a time from level 5 [TV Tropes (listing)]. Wild or not: not found.
- **Locks.** A padlocked Chuzzle freezes its row and column until it is matched;
  later levels have more. [Wikipedia: Chuzzle; All The Tropes]
- **Flask.** The eyes of popped Chuzzles fly into a flask beside the board
  (reviews also say beaker). Six or more at once fill it much faster. A full
  flask ends the level, and the eyes rush out. [Wikipedia: Chuzzle; All The
  Tropes; Gamezebo: Chuzzle]
- **Scoring and lives.** Bigger pops and cascades score more. The level bonus is
  1,000 × level on Casual and 2,000 × level on Expert, capped at 10,000. A hint
  costs points and flask progress. With no moves left a "scramble" reshuffles
  the board; the game ends when scrambles run out, and one more comes every
  300,000 points. [Wikipedia: Chuzzle]
- **Modes.** Classic; Speed (a lock timer fills while nothing pops); Zen
  (endless, no locks); Mind Bender (20 levels of five puzzles: arrange the board
  to a pattern); each at Casual or Expert. A trophy room rewards feats such as
  seven popped at once. [Wikipedia: Chuzzle; Gamezebo: Chuzzle]
- **Look.** "Googly-eyed balls of fur" [Infopackets]; "basically just coloured
  balls with eyes" [The Next Web]; "pom-poms or Tribbles" [Pocket-lint]. They
  have no limbs; on PC some wear huge sunglasses [All The Tropes]. Not found:
  3D-rendered fur or not, the eye style, hair bows, fur left after a pop.
- **Behaviors.** They blink as neighbors slide [Pocket Gamer: Chuzzle], and
  their eyes follow the pointer [Backloggd (listing)]. Clicked repeatedly, one
  coughs, sneezes, then sneezes its fur off for a couple of seconds; a Fat
  Chuzzle belches. Hovered on too long, one gets mad and shakes the cursor off.
  A row dragged back and forth gets dizzy. Left alone, they fall asleep one by
  one with Zs. In Casual, a playable Chuzzle rolls its eyes, then twitches, as a
  hint. [All The Tropes; Gamezebo: Chuzzle]
- **Sound.** They "squeak" and "squeal" [Gamezebo: Chuzzle], are "sneezing and
  giggling" and pop "with a chipmunk-alike noise" [Pocket-lint]. The music is
  "1970s-inspired discotheque" [Gamezebo: Chuzzle], sold as a "Funky Soundtrack
  With Retro Sound Effects" [Infopackets]. The flask's sound recalls Pac-Man's
  eyes [All The Tropes].
- **Praise.** Pocket Gamer (4.5 of 5): "the game just feels crafted", with
  "cute" pieces "blinking every so often". The Next Web: "simple to grasp,
  charming and addictive", with no time limit. PC Magazine gave 4/5 and Gamezebo
  80/100. [Pocket Gamer: Chuzzle; The Next Web; Wikipedia: Chuzzle; Gamezebo:
  Chuzzle]
- **Criticism.** "There's just no way to foresee when you'll run out of moves"
  [Gamezebo: Chuzzle]. Pocket-lint called it an obvious Bejeweled clone and
  "Annoying, addictive and absorbing" [Pocket-lint].
- **Sequels.** Chuzzle 2 adds level goals, eggs, "stinkers", a daily challenge
  and the Chuzzarium, a pet house; GiN gave it 4/5 and disliked only the ads,
  and version 5.5 fixed slowdown in long sessions. In Chuzzle Snap (3.9 from 25
  ratings) the player places set pieces instead of sliding lines. [App Store:
  Chuzzle 2; Steam: Chuzzle 2; GiN; App Store: Chuzzle Snap]

## 2. Traits distinctive to Chuzzle

Each item is my reading of sections 1 and 5 (inferred). Any one of them, set on
fuzzy balls with eyes, points at Chuzzle.

- **Name.** A coined "-uzzle" word that rhymes with "puzzle".
- **Move and board.** Dragging whole rows and columns that wrap around, on 6×6
  with eight colors.
- **Meter.** Eyes that leave the body and fly into a flask or beaker; a
  "test-tube home".
- **Specials.** A creature grown to 2×2; a flashing creature, made from five,
  that blows up 3×3; a rainbow creature worth a bonus.
- **Locks.** Padlocks on creatures that freeze a row and a column.
- **Structure.** "Scrambles" as lives; Classic, Speed, Zen and Mind Bender at
  Casual or Expert; a trophy room.
- **Eyes.** Googly eyes that track the pointer, and an eye roll as the hint.
- **Poke reactions.** Cough, sneeze, fur blown off, belch, anger at a hovering
  cursor, dizziness when shaken, sleeping with Zs.
- **Voice.** Squeaks, squeals, giggles, sneezes and a chipmunk pop, over disco
  and funk.
- **Extras.** Sunglasses on some pieces, and a virtual pet room.

## 3. Other games with living pieces

- **Puyo Puyo.** Puyo are "round, slime-like creatures" from the Madō Monogatari
  RPGs. Four or more of one color joined up, down or sideways pop, and chains
  send colorless garbage Puyo to the rival. The usual colors are red, green,
  blue, yellow and purple. A cracked Hard Puyo's eyes float up out of the field.
  Their look when joined: not found. [Wikipedia: Puyo Puyo; Puyo Nexus]
- **Dr. Mario.** Red, yellow and blue viruses stay put until four in a line with
  capsule halves remove them. They are pests, so clearing them is a cure;
  parents still objected to the medicine premise. [Wikipedia: Dr. Mario]
- **Yoshi's Cookie.** The player rotates whole lines of a grid, like a Rubik's
  Cube, to fill a row or column with one cookie. A remake dates from 2003, so
  line sliding is older than Chuzzle (inferred). [Wikipedia: Yoshi's Cookie]
- **Zoo Keeper (2003).** A swap match-three of animal tiles (lion, giraffe,
  panda, elephant, crocodile, gorilla) in "Lego block" graphics, with a quota
  per animal. The fiction is recapturing escaped zoo animals. When time runs out
  they "rattle in excitement". [Wikipedia: Zoo Keeper; Pocket Gamer: Zoo Keeper]
- **Pokémon Trozei and Shuffle.** In Trozei lines slide and wrap, and four in a
  row clear. Its fiction is rescue: a beamer sends matched sets of stolen
  Pokémon to a professor. In Shuffle, matches damage a wild Pokémon that may
  then be caught. [Wikipedia: Pokémon Trozei; Wikipedia: Pokémon Shuffle]
- **Disney Tsum Tsum and Emoji Blitz.** Tsum Tsum's pieces are stackable plush
  versions of Disney characters, five kinds on screen; the player draws a line
  through three or more neighbors to pop them. Kotaku called it "rather
  repetitive". Emoji Blitz uses emoji of about 400 characters. [Kotaku: Tsum
  Tsum; Wikipedia: Emoji Blitz]
- **Angry Birds matchers.** Blast pops balloons, some bird-shaped, and was
  called "generic". Dream Blast pops "dream bubbles", and birds are power-ups.
  [Wikipedia: Angry Birds Blast; App Store: Dream Blast (listing)]
- **Best Fiends.** Tiles are leaves and mushrooms, not creatures. The bug heroes
  stand beside it, powered by the colors matched. [Pocket Gamer: Best Fiends]
- **Pet Rescue Saga.** Pieces are plain colored blocks. Pets ride on top and are
  saved when they reach the bottom; the villains are "pet snatchers". [App
  Store: Pet Rescue (listing)]
- **Farm Heroes Saga.** Matched "cropsies" count toward a quota. A "grumpy"
  cropsie looks angry, with mud and a pink glow; a match that includes one
  scores nothing, and grass turns it back. [Farm Heroes wiki (listing)]
- **Threes.** Number tiles have voices and small faces beneath the card. "Each
  kind of number tile has its own personality" (384 is a pirate). Early themes
  (sushi, animals, a monster that ate tiles) confused testers and were dropped
  for plain numbers. [Wikipedia: Threes]
- **Triple Town.** Bears wander and block squares. A trapped bear becomes a
  gravestone, and three gravestones make a church. [Wikipedia: Triple Town]

## 4. Fuzzy matchers and fuzzball mascots

- **Fuzzy Flip - Matching Game (2023).** "Colorful and huggable" Fuzzies on
  "multi-hued cubes": tap to match or merge, 150 levels, no timer. By Cateia
  Games; 3 Steam reviews. [Steam: Fuzzy Flip]
- **Fuzzy Balls (Les Ganton, 2014).** "Match colored Fuzzy Balls to free your
  trapped friends." [TouchArcade (listing)]
- **Fuzzle Pop (2011, iPhone).** "Match three fuzzles in the same color and
  they'll pop." Developer and availability: not found. [VideoGameGeek (listing)]
- **Fuzzle (2008, iOS).** Slide colored balls into lines of five. Its colorblind
  option tells colors apart by the orientation of a symbol. [App Store: Fuzzle
  Lite]
- **Not found.** No matcher named Color Fuzzies, Bubble Fuzzy, Furballs, Pompom,
  Wooly or Frizzle Fraz appeared in the searches run.
- **Susuwatari (Studio Ghibli).** Golf-ball-sized, pitch-black and fuzzy, with
  two large eyes and thin limbs that extend for work. They make a "squeaky
  murmuring sound". [Wikipedia: Susuwatari]
- **Fuzzy (Super Mario World, 1990).** Black, spiky, cross-eyed and toothy;
  later games add green, pink and gold kinds. [Super Mario Wiki: Fuzzy]
- **Fuzzles (Oddworld: Munch's Oddysee, 2001).** A "puffball-like species" of
  lab animals that the player frees. [Wikipedia: Munch's Oddysee]
- **Tribbles (Star Trek).** "Small bundles of fur with no other visible
  features" that coo and multiply. Their writer first called them "fuzzies" and
  renamed them to avoid H. Beam Piper's novel Little Fuzzy. [Wikipedia: Tribble]
- **Koosh.** A toy ball of about 2,000 rubber filaments (1987). Koosh Kins added
  faces and hands. [Wikipedia: Koosh ball]
- **Puffles (Club Penguin, Disney).** Small, round, furry, limbless pets with a
  personality per color (red bold, white shy). [Puffle wiki (listing)]

## 5. How games frame clearing creatures

- **Send them home.** Steam's text for Chuzzle Deluxe says matched Chuzzles "pop
  and return to their test-tube home". [Steam: Chuzzle Deluxe]
- **Call it joy.** Chuzzle 2's "explode with delight" and Snap's "explode with
  happiness". Reviews of Chuzzle still write "vaporize", "eyeballs hop into a
  beaker" and "exploding furry pom-poms": a happy label does not hide what the
  animation shows (inferred). [App Store: Chuzzle 2; App Store: Chuzzle Snap;
  Gamezebo: Chuzzle; Pocket-lint]
- **Rescue or collect.** Trozei beams stolen Pokémon to safety, Fuzzy Balls
  frees trapped friends, Zoo Keeper recaptures escapees and Farm Heroes collects
  toward a quota. [Wikipedia: Pokémon Trozei; TouchArcade (listing); Pocket
  Gamer: Zoo Keeper; Farm Heroes wiki (listing)]
- **Keep creatures off the board.** Pet Rescue clears blocks and saves the pets
  that ride them, Best Fiends matches leaves while the creatures stand at the
  side, and Dream Blast makes birds the reward. [App Store: Pet Rescue
  (listing); Pocket Gamer: Best Fiends; App Store: Dream Blast (listing)]
- **Make them toys or pests.** Tsum Tsum's pieces are plush toys and Dr. Mario's
  are viruses. [Kotaku: Tsum Tsum; Wikipedia: Dr. Mario]
- **Merge, or lean in.** In Threes two tiles become one new character. Triple
  Town turns trapped bears into gravestones, a dark joke that suits a strategy
  game (inferred). [Wikipedia: Threes; Wikipedia: Triple Town]
- **Criticism of harm.** No review objecting to clearing cute pieces was found.
  Nearest: parents objected to Dr. Mario's pills. [Wikipedia: Dr. Mario]

## 6. What charms and what annoys

- **Small, rare motion charms.** The trait a reviewer singles out in Chuzzle is
  a blink "every so often" while neighbors move; plain balls with eyes read as
  "really characterful". [Pocket Gamer: Chuzzle; The Next Web]
- **Optional mischief.** Chuzzle's poke reactions are something to do "if you
  get bored", never needed for play. [Gamezebo: Chuzzle]
- **Faces that carry game state.** Threes' tiles emote when paired, Farm Heroes
  shows a scoring rule as a grumpy face, and Chuzzle's eye roll is a hint.
  [Wikipedia: Threes; Farm Heroes wiki (listing); All The Tropes]
- **Voices divide.** TouchArcade says to play Threes with sound on; CNET found
  its music repetitive and the voices "a little creepy" without it.
  Pocket-lint's first word for Chuzzle is "Annoying". [Wikipedia: Threes;
  Pocket-lint]
- **Seizing the pointer.** Chuzzle shakes the cursor off a piece hovered on too
  long, which interrupts aiming (inferred). Eyes that follow a pointer have
  nothing to follow on a phone (inferred). [All The Tropes]
- **Readability.** Threes kept numbers large and faces small, after themes that
  replaced numbers confused testers. [Wikipedia: Threes]
- **Performance.** Chuzzle 2 needed a fix for stutter in long sessions; many
  animated pieces cost frames (inferred). [App Store: Chuzzle 2]
- **Luck annoys more than cuteness.** Gamezebo's main complaint was dead boards
  the player cannot foresee. [Gamezebo: Chuzzle]

## 7. Telling six kinds apart

- **The numbers.** Red-green difficulty affects "around 8-10% of males". The
  guideline asks for a second signifier (shape, symbol, pattern or text) before
  any palette filter, and names Two Dots and Puzzle Retreat as examples. [Game
  Accessibility Guidelines]
- **Chuzzle.** Color alone separates eight kinds; the sunglasses are not
  reported as tied to a color. A colorblind mode: not found. [Wikipedia:
  Chuzzle; All The Tropes]
- **Puyo Puyo.** Color alone by default. Puyo Puyo Tetris had letter-marked
  "Alphabet Puyos" that cost in-game credits, so colorblind players struggled
  until they could afford them. Puyo Puyo Tetris 2 added settings for three
  kinds of color blindness in 2021. [AbleGamers (listing); Nintendo Life]
- **Fuzzle (2008).** A symbol on each ball, turned a different way for each
  color: cheap, and it keeps one silhouette (inferred). [App Store: Fuzzle Lite]
- **Different species or objects.** Zoo Keeper, Tsum Tsum and Trozei give each
  kind its own animal or character; Best Fiends and Farm Heroes use leaf,
  mushroom and crop outlines. Color is then a bonus (inferred). [Wikipedia: Zoo
  Keeper; Kotaku: Tsum Tsum; Pocket Gamer: Best Fiends]
- **Same creature, different temper.** Puffles tie a personality to each color;
  Mario's Fuzzy kinds differ by color only. [Puffle wiki (listing); Super Mario
  Wiki: Fuzzy]
- **Added features.** Koosh Kins and Threes build character with hands, a tooth
  or an eyepatch. A creature with no limbs or mouth has only its fur outline,
  its eye shape and one prop to vary (inferred). [Wikipedia: Koosh ball;
  Wikipedia: Threes]
- **Small sizes.** On a phone board eight cells wide a cell is about 45 px.
  Strands vanish there; an outline and one bold mark survive (inferred).

- **Fuzz Bunch.** No game, app, toy or book was found. As one word, "Fuzzbunch"
  is the exploit framework leaked by the Shadow Brokers in April 2017 with
  EternalBlue. The field is different, so confusion of source is unlikely, but a
  search for the name returns malware write-ups, and security filters may match
  the string (inferred). [Qualys (listing)]

- **Fluffle.** "Fluffle: Bunny Idle Game" (iOS, 2024, 327 ratings, updated

  coined on Wikipedia in 2007, and a creature in the Dizzy games bears it. [App
  Store: Fluffle; Wiktionary; Wikipedia: Fluffle]
- **Fuzzle.** Found: Fuzzle (iOS puzzle, 2008, 365 ratings on the free version);
  Fuzzle Pop (2011); Oddworld's Fuzzles; a 2026 Charlie Bears plush bear named
  Fuzzle; live FUZZLES registrations for plush toys (one held by Douglas

  will crowd searches (inferred). [Steam: Fuzzy Flip]
- **Fuzz Shuffle.** No exact hit, but "Shuffle" already names two match-three
  games, Pokémon Shuffle and Fluffy Shuffle (iOS, 2015). [Wikipedia: Pokémon

- **Triple Fuzz Pop and Fuzz Pop.** No exact hit for either. Near names: Fuzzle

## 9. What this suggests for Fuzz Bunch

1. Keep the swap of two neighbors and never slide a whole line. Line sliding
   with wraparound is the first thing every description of Chuzzle names.
2. Pick a board that is not 6×6, with six kinds or fewer. Chuzzle is 6×6 with
   eight colors, and Tsum Tsum keeps five kinds on screen.
3. Keep every fuzzball whole when it leaves. Eyes flying into a flask are
   Chuzzle's most recognizable image, which reviewers describe in grisly words.
4. Frame a match as three friends going off together (a hop, a huddle, a roll
   off the board), and never write "pop", "explode" or "burst" of a fuzzball.
   Gentle games rescue, collect or send home, and "explode with delight" still
   reads as an explosion.
5. Use no flask, beaker, bottle, test tube or laboratory for the progress meter.
   A count of gathered trios or a filling nest says the same without it.
6. Do not let eyes follow the pointer. It is a Chuzzle signature and does
   nothing on touch; a glance at a same-colored neighbor says more.
7. Leave out the poke reactions: sneeze, fur loss, belch, anger at hovering,
   dizziness, sleeping with Zs. Any idle behavior should be new and should never
   move the pointer or block input.
8. Do not copy the special set: no 2×2 fat creature, no flashing "super"
   creature that blasts 3×3, no rainbow creature, no padlocks that freeze lines.
   Build specials from fuzzball traits, such as a fluffed-up fuzzball, with
   their own names.
9. Avoid Classic, Speed, Zen and Mind Bender as a set of mode names, "scrambles"
   as lives, and a trophy room. Together they are Chuzzle's menu.
10. Keep sound non-vocal by default: soft plucks or chimes that rise through a
    cascade, with no squeaks, giggles, sneezes, pitched-up pops or disco. Voices
    are what reviewers call annoying or creepy, and they are the core of
    Chuzzle's sound.
11. Animate sparingly: a rare blink, a lean toward a match, a small bounce on
    landing, all still under reduced motion. The praised detail in Chuzzle is an
    occasional blink, and Chuzzle 2 had to fix stutter.
12. Give each of the six kinds its own outline as well as its color, for example
    spiky, curly, droopy, tufted, cropped and wavy fur. Up to a tenth of men
    cannot rely on red against green, and an outline survives a 45 px cell.
13. Offer a symbol or pattern option from the first screen, never as an unlock.
    Puyo Puyo Tetris made players earn its letter-marked pieces, and Fuzzle's
    turned symbol shows how little is needed.
14. Keep the two dark oval eyes the same across kinds and let fur carry
    identity. Threes kept faces small so numbers stayed readable, and flat art
    without white googly eyes already sets the pieces apart from Chuzzle's.
15. Use expression for rules, sparingly: neighbors perk up when one swap from a
    trio. Do not borrow Farm Heroes' "grumpy" state by name or by look.
16. Do not make blockers out of creatures that must be harmed. Triple Town's
    gravestones belong to another tone; tangles, burrs or lint that a fuzzball
    shakes off fit a gentle game.
17. Avoid "Fuzzle" and "Fluffle" outright, and prefer not to use "Fuzz Friends",
    "Fuzz Match", "Fuzz Shuffle" or "Triple Fuzz Pop". The first two are taken,
    and the others sit in crowded or generic company.
18. "Fuzz Trio", "Fuzz Huddle" and "Fuzz Flock" look safest, with "Tuft Luck"
    and "Fuzz Muddle" also clear. "Fuzz Bunch" is usable but shares its letters
    with a notorious hacking tool. Run the store and trademark searches that
    could not be run here before choosing.
