# Catalog lines and variant write-ups

A catalog is the list inside How to play that shows each brick, capsule, rock or
pickup: a picture, a name, one line. A variant write-up is the line that says
what an option or a rule variant changes.

## Lines to copy

From shipped games:

- "Fires at the nearest enemy." (Vampire Survivors)
- "Enlarges the bar." (KBreakOut)
- "Gives the player an additional life." (KBreakOut)
- "Transports items forward." (Mindustry)
- "Deal 6 damage." (Slay the Spire)
- "+4 Mult" (Balatro)

From these games, as they should read:

- Steel brick: "Can't be broken. Balls bounce off it, and it blocks laser
  bolts."
- Tough brick: "Takes 2 to 4 hits. The pips show how many are left."
- Regrowing brick: "Grows back after 10 seconds. You don't need to clear it."
- Wide paddle: "Makes the paddle 50% wider for 20 seconds."
- Catch: "Balls stick to the paddle for up to 3 seconds, or until you launch
  them. Lasts 20 seconds."
- Bumper: "Can't be broken. Knocks the fuzzball away and drains its charge."
- Prism: "Splits the fuzzball into 3."
- Spread: "Fires 3 shots at once for 12 seconds."
- Burr: "Homes in on your ship and sticks to it, slowing you down. Shoot it
  first, or dash to shake it off."

## The shape

**The thing is the subject, and you leave it out.** Start with the verb: Takes,
Fires, Splits, Makes, Blocks, Grows back. This is the grammar most games use,
and the old lines here already had it.

**12 words. Never over 20.** One sentence. A second sentence is for a number the
player needs ("Lasts 20 seconds.") or the one catch ("You don't need to clear
it."). If there are two catches, choose the one that changes how you play.

**Digits, and the unit.** "20 seconds", "50%", "3 shots", "2 to 4 hits".

**Something to do goes to "you".** "Shoot it first, or dash to shake it off."

**The same verb for the same thing** all the way down a catalog. If one brick
"Takes 2 hits", the next does not "need 3 strikes". If one unbreakable brick
says "Can't be broken", they all do.

**Leave the edge cases out.** What a zap does to a prism, the angle between
split shots, the order two effects resolve in. A player sees these happen and
does not need them in words.

**Have fun with the name. Keep the line plain.** Bumper, Burr and Prism are good
names. Their lines say what they do and nothing else.

## Names

One or two words. A thing the player can see (Steel brick, Wide paddle) or an
image that fits it (Burr, Prism). The first word has a capital and the rest do
not, unless the game shows it otherwise. Check that the name is not already
taken by something else in the same game.

Level, wave and sector names follow the same rule: short, and the kind of thing
a person would call the picture in front of them. "Stripes", "Arch", "First
light".

## Variant and option write-ups

20 words. Say what changes, compared with the game the player already knows.
Start from the change, and talk to "you".

- "Vegas: you pay $52 for the deck and win $5 for each card on a foundation. 1
  pass through the stock in Draw 1, 3 in Draw 3."
- "Kings Only: only a King can fill an empty column. That also limits how many
  cards you can move at once."
- "Relaxed: a slower ball, and no capsules that make things harder."

When one write-up covers several variants, give each its own sentence that
starts with its name. Do not chain them with semicolons.
